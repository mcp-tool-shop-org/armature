## H1 — Stage A, the solver against mathematics

**NOT BLIND.** I am about to write the construction, and it is exact by design: each bone's
rotation is built to carry its rest direction onto the observed direction, so the position
round-trip is an algebraic identity in float64 rather than a fit.

- **H1a — position round-trip.** Max site error < **1e-9** of the bbox diagonal, over every
  frame, when the observation is generated by the rig's own kinematics inside the solver's
  parameterisation.
- **H1b — rotation round-trip on observable DOFs.** Max geodesic error < **1e-9 rad**.
- **H1c — the unobservable DOFs are named, not silently zeroed.** Every bone whose twist the
  positions do not determine is flagged per frame. Predicted flagged-always: `wrist.L`,
  `wrist.R`, `ankle.L`, `ankle.R` (no child landmark past their tail). Predicted flagged only
  when the limb is straight: `shoulder.*`, `elbow.*`, `hip.*`, `knee.*`.

A miss on H1a or H1b is a bug in the solver, never a tuning target.

## H2 — Stage B1, detection on a stylized mannequin

**BLIND. Written before MediaPipe was installed and before the first frame was rendered.**
I have not seen a single detector output on this subject, and the spec calls this genuinely
uncertain. Three clauses, predicted separately.

- **H2a — it fires.** The Pose Landmarker returns a pose on **≥ 90 %** of the 65 frames
  (≥ 59 frames). Reasoning: the subject is a full-body humanoid silhouette, single person,
  head visible, near the camera — the card's own in-scope envelope — and BlazePose's person
  detector keys on gross body layout rather than on surface detail.
- **H2b — visibility sits mid-range, not high.** Mean visibility across all 33 landmarks on
  fired frames lands in **0.50–0.90**.
- **H2c — the head landmarks are the weak ones.** Mean visibility over the eight face
  landmarks (nose, six eye points, two mouth corners) is **lower** than mean visibility over
  the four torso landmarks (two shoulders, two hips). Reasoning: the mesh carries no eye, ear
  or mouth feature at all — E07's landmark derivation says so in writing — so any face
  landmark is regressed from a blank head.

**If H2a misses, that is a full negative result for this fixture class** and the stage stops
there. I am not predicting which way that would be "better".

## H3 — Stage B1, how far the solved rotations land from the authored ones

**BLIND on magnitude.** Conditional on H2a firing.

- **H3a — the error is large in absolute terms.** Median per-joint geodesic error over the 17
  gait bones × fired frames is **> 10°**.
- **H3b — the legs are worse than the arms.** Median error on `hip.*`/`knee.*`/`ankle.*`
  exceeds median error on `shoulder.*`/`elbow.*`/`wrist.*`.
- **H3c — bone-length residual is not small.** Median |residual| over the 14 limb bones
  exceeds **10 %** of rest length, because MediaPipe regresses a generic adult body and the
  performer is a mannequin with his own proportions.
- **H3d — the model gap alone is smaller than the detector gap.** Feeding the *authored*
  ground-truth landmark positions (no detector at all) through the same solver gives a median
  per-joint error **at least 3× smaller** than feeding the detected ones. This separates
  "the solver's model cannot represent the walk" from "the detector is noisy," and it costs
  nothing to run.

## What would make me wrong in a way I would not notice

Recorded because it is the useful half.

- If MediaPipe fires on **some** frames and the solver silently interpolates the rest, every
  number above would be computed on a population I did not describe. The detection gate raises
  before any metric exists, and the fired-frame count is carried beside every mean.
- If the MediaPipe world-landmark axis convention is not what I assume, the solved rotations
  would be wrong in a *consistent* direction and would still round-trip perfectly. The
  convention is therefore **measured** against the known ground truth rather than assumed, and
  both the assumed-convention residual and the best-fit-rotation residual are reported.
- If the detector locks onto the render's background or a shadow rather than the figure, the
  visibility numbers would look plausible. The gate reports per-landmark presence, and the
  sheet carries the overlay so the Director can see where the detector thought he was.
